Capnogram: phases and abnormal shapesDiagram
  • Phase I: anatomic dead space (no CO2). Phase II: mixing of dead space and alveolar gas. Phase III: alveolar plateau, ending at the end-tidal value. Phase 0: inspiration.
  • Dashed trace: prolonged upstroke and steep plateau ("shark fin") of bronchospasm, COPD or a kinked tube.
  • Baseline above zero: rebreathing from exhausted absorbent (corrects with high fresh gas flow) or an incompetent unidirectional valve (does not).
  • Notch in the plateau ("curare cleft"): spontaneous effort during recovery from relaxant. Cardiogenic oscillations: small ripples in time with the pulse.
  • Sudden zero: esophageal intubation, disconnection, total obstruction. Exponential fall: embolism, arrest or severe hypotension. Gradual rise: hypoventilation, malignant hyperthermia, CO2 absorption in laparoscopy, tourniquet or clamp release.
  • Normal PaCO2 to ETCO2 gradient is 2 to 5 mmHg; it widens with dead space (embolism, low output, COPD).
CVP waveform and its relation to the ECGDiagram
  • a wave: atrial contraction, follows the P wave. c wave: tricuspid valve bulging into the atrium at the start of ventricular systole, follows the QRS.
  • x descent: atrial relaxation and downward movement of the tricuspid annulus during systole.
  • v wave: venous filling of the atrium against a closed tricuspid valve, peaks after the T wave. y descent: tricuspid opening and early ventricular filling.
  • No a wave: atrial fibrillation. Cannon a waves: atrium contracting against a closed valve (junctional rhythm, complete heart block, ventricular pacing).
  • Large v (cv) waves: tricuspid regurgitation. Prominent x and y descents: constrictive pericarditis. Blunted y descent with raised pressure: tamponade.
  • Read the CVP at end expiration, at the base of the c wave (end diastole).
Oxyhemoglobin dissociation curveDiagram
  • Solid: normal adult curve. Dots mark the landmarks: PO2 27 = 50% (P50 26.7 mmHg), 40 = 75% (mixed venous), 60 = 90%.
  • Right shift (red, higher P50, easier unloading): acidosis, hypercarbia, hyperthermia, raised 2,3-DPG (chronic hypoxia, anemia, altitude), volatile anesthetics slightly, sickle hemoglobin.
  • Left shift (blue, lower P50, tighter binding): alkalosis, hypocarbia, hypothermia, low 2,3-DPG (stored blood), fetal hemoglobin (P50 19), carboxyhemoglobin, methemoglobin.
  • Bohr effect: CO2 and hydrogen ion lower hemoglobin oxygen affinity. Haldane effect: deoxygenated hemoglobin carries more CO2.
  • Below a PO2 of 60 the curve is steep: small falls in PO2 cause large falls in saturation.

LAST: checklistTable
StepAction
1Stop injecting; call for help and the LAST kit; consider alerting the bypass or ECMO team
2Airway: 100% oxygen, ventilate; avoid hypoxia and acidosis, which worsen toxicity
3Seizures: benzodiazepine preferred; small doses of propofol only if stable
420% lipid emulsion early. Over 70 kg: 100 mL bolus over 2-3 min, then about 250 mL over 15-20 min. Under 70 kg: 1.5 mL/kg bolus, then 0.25 mL/kg/min
5Still unstable: repeat the bolus once or twice and double the infusion. Maximum about 12 mL/kg
6Arrest: CPR with epinephrine at 1 mcg/kg or less per dose; amiodarone for ventricular arrhythmia
7Avoid vasopressin, calcium channel blockers, beta blockers and any local anesthetic including lidocaine
8Continue lipid at least 15 min after stability; monitor 2 h after a seizure, 4-6 h after cardiovascular instability
  • Presentation: perioral numbness, metallic taste, tinnitus, agitation, seizure, then conduction block, bradycardia, ventricular arrhythmia, asystole. Bupivacaine can present with cardiac collapse first.
  • Risk: extremes of age, low muscle mass, cardiac, hepatic or renal disease, pregnancy, high-absorption sites.

Choosing a statistical testTable
DataTwo groupsThree or more groupsPaired / repeated
Continuous, normalUnpaired t-testANOVAPaired t-test / repeated-measures ANOVA
Ordinal or skewedMann-Whitney UKruskal-WallisWilcoxon signed-rank / Friedman
CategoricalChi-square (Fisher exact if small)Chi-squareMcNemar
Time to eventLog-rank (Kaplan-Meier)Cox regression
  • Correlation: Pearson (normal), Spearman (ranked). Regression: linear for continuous outcomes, logistic for binary.
  • Sensitivity = TP/(TP+FN); specificity = TN/(TN+FP); PPV and NPV vary with prevalence. Likelihood ratio positive = sensitivity / (1 - specificity).
  • Type I error (alpha) = false positive; type II (beta) = false negative; power = 1 - beta. Intention-to-treat preserves randomization.
  • Study designs: case-control gives odds ratios (rare outcomes); cohort gives relative risk; randomized trials control confounding; meta-analysis pools trials (forest plot, heterogeneity I-squared).
